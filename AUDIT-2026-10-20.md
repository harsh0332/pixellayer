# PixelLayerr experience audit — 20 October 2026

## Findings and implementation

- **Homepage services were hidden behind a text accordion.** Six visible service cards now use small illustrations that explain websites, storefronts, products, automation, business systems and redesign. The services index shows all ten offers; detail pages have selectable, pausable walkthroughs and concrete review checkpoints.
- **The homepage offered little depth after the portfolio.** La Vallée Farms and The Sugar Story now have detailed journey explanations, live screenshot links and contextual enquiry links. The full 15-project scroll portfolio still appears only once. An attention-to-enquiry section and ownership/handover section give visitors more useful reasons to continue.
- **The Sugar Story preview was stale.** Its current storefront was inspected and captured from the browser. The updated asset excludes the logged-in administration UI. Copy describes observed product discovery, custom cakes and the dessert hamper builder. It is labelled e-commerce, without claiming that this WooCommerce project runs on Shopify.
- **Process information needed a tangible review path.** Four distinct illustrations show discovery, design, development and handover. The approach page explains what the studio shares and what the client reviews at each milestone.
- **Enquiry links lost project context.** Valid service and project identifiers now preselect the relevant need and add a project reference to the brief. Unknown identifiers are ignored. The form label now covers a current website or project reference; its WhatsApp brief uses the correct public domain.
- **Testimonial claims lacked supporting evidence in this workspace.** The quotation carousel and associated Review structured data were removed. Factual scope, ownership and review information replaces them; no conversion uplift is asserted.
- **Motion could restart while keyboard focus remained inside a walkthrough.** Pointer and focus pause flags are now independent. Manual pause, reduced-motion preferences, visibility and document-hidden state all stop automatic progression. The approved Resolution hero is preserved, with a clearer supporting sentence.

## Validation

- Node 22 production build using Webpack: passed, 41 generated routes.
- ESLint and `git diff --check`: passed.
- All 32 sitemap pages and 103 unique internal destinations/anchors returned valid results on the production preview.
- `scripts/audit-journeys.mjs`: homepage proof, all ten service detail pages, project links, valid/invalid enquiry selections, process content and removal of Review structured data passed.
- `scripts/audit-preview.mjs`: served CSS, approved hero identity, one complete portfolio and linked page assets passed.
- `scripts/check-resolution-hero.mjs`: existing canvas lifecycle and rendering-budget checks passed using a mock DOM/canvas.
- `scripts/check-studio-sequence.mjs`: focus/hover independence, manual selection, global pause, reduced motion, visibility and cleanup passed using deterministic hook/timer/observer doubles.
- Production dependency audit: zero reported vulnerabilities. Development-only upstream findings remain documented in `DEPLOYMENT.md`; dependencies were not changed in this experience update.

## Verification boundaries

The live reference and Sugar Story capture were inspected earlier in this audit. Browser access subsequently became unavailable. The final changed pages have **not** received a rendered desktop/mobile check or a real-browser interaction check: the tool still reports that local browser use is disabled after the user enabled access. The lifecycle and HTTP checks above do not replace that visual check. No enquiry or third-party booking was submitted during testing.
